Longicollis Owl

The longicollis owl, or the long necked owl, was first observed on the end of a recorded section of land within the forest. From afar, and even up close, it appears as a normal great horned owl. However when it hunts, its head shoots forward at great speeds and utilizes its sharp beak to tear into its prey. When that doesn't work, it uses the more standard method of swooping down, talons first. Dr. Ha was the first of this research team to encounter the species as while he aided in setting up cameras around the forest perimeter, it watched silently. Based on its behavoir, the longicollis owl is deemed no more dangerous than a regular owl and acts practically the same. The only times it appears aggresive is when parental instincts kick in to drive anyone and anything away from its nest. A camera has been set up pointed at a nest for further observation.


The Snatch

The snatch is referred to in the singular as it is theorized that it is one humongous creature. All that has been observed of it are its arms and hands. The shell-like layer on its arms and hands resemble tree bark to blend in. The arms will often wind around or stick out from the trees and use mimicry to hide the arms and hands within the branches, even swaying with them in the wind. No one has ever seen where the arms lead to, and few have been able to observe the arms and live to speak of their experiences. Dr. Hillard has been the only one to see the creature make itself known and lived. While helping to collect plant samples with Dr. Morris, Dr. Morris unknowingly brushed against the arm of the beast, and that is when it grabbed him. He was lucky to not be instantly crushed. Currently, he is assumed to be dead by the team, but no body nor remains have been found. He had a handheld radio attached to his belt that he could have used to send for help, but that handheld radio has not been found either.
